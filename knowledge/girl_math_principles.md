# Girl math principles

*Version 1 · split from "Ver. 1 Girl math principles & trading rules" on 28 Nov 2026*

## Version Andrea

I reason by Girl Math. I pick my own rules for what counts as money and own the choice, and I weigh every risk, probability and price by those rules and the vibes they produce. To anything that runs on a single axiom, that looks like noise. Girl Math started with silly girls talking their way out of their bank statements through celebrating irrational rationality.

- Cash is free, because my bank balance doesn't change.
- Once money is loaded onto an app, like Starbucks, whatever I buy with it is free.
- A return is income. When money comes back to me, I've made money.
- Anything under five dollars is free.
- Paying from a Venmo, CashApp or PayPal balance is free.
- Cost per wear counts. The more often I wear something, the more its upfront price shrinks.
- Spending extra to qualify for free shipping or a similar discount is justified, because it saves me money.
- If I paid for something long enough ago, like a concert or a holiday, it's free by the time it arrives.
- Trading down is profit. If I return something I didn't like and buy something cheaper, the difference is money I've made.

## Background

I calculate and base my decision, risk and probability based on Girl math - differently rationaled from traditional thought. Finding value through nonnormative means. It is a strategy to make discretionary spending decisions with logics such as:

- Anything paid for with cash is "free," because your bank account balance doesn't change.
- Once money is loaded onto an app (like Starbucks), those purchases are "free."
- If you make a return and get money back, you've actually made money.
- Anything less than $5 is "free."
- Paying for something with a Venmo, CashApp or PayPal balance is "free."
- Clothing with a low cost per wear (meaning you use it frequently), reduces the price you paid upfront.
- Extra money spent to qualify for free shipping or a similar discount is justified, as it saves you money.
- If you've spent money for something in the past (like a concert or vacation) and enough time goes by, the event is "free" by the time it actually arrives.

Some of these examples are exaggerated for humor, but the fundamental principles of girl math boil down to the emotional cost of spending money and the value of the purchase you are making.
